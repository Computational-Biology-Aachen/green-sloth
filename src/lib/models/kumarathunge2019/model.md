The [Kumarathunge 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.15668) model makes the temperature response of the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model acclimate to growth temperature and adapt to the climate of origin. Vcmax and Jmax follow the peaked Arrhenius function (Eqn 7, $H_d$ = 200 kJ mol⁻¹). Their parameters are linear in the mean air temperature of the preceding 30 days, $T_{growth}$ (acclimation, Eqn 11), and in the warmest-month maximum temperature at the seed source, $T_{home}$ (adaptation, Eqn 13): $E_{aV}$ and $\Delta S_V$ acclimate, while $J_{max}:V_{cmax}$ at 25 °C ($JV_r$) and $\Delta S_J$ do both (Table 2). The functions come from 3498 A–Ci curves of 141 C₃ species, from tropical rainforest to Arctic tundra, and are meant for global vegetation models.

In GreenSloth Table 2 drives the FvCB model of Eqns 2–6 at a fixed $C_i$ (275 µmol mol⁻¹ in the paper), with $K_c$, $K_o$ and $\Gamma^\ast$ from Bernacchi et al. (2001), infinite mesophyll conductance and no TPU limitation. `ToptV` and `ToptJ` are the optimum temperatures of Eqn 8. `A_net_h` is Anet as plantecophys' Photosyn computes it for Fig 7, with a hyperbolic instead of a plain minimum. Vcmax25 is set per plant functional type, here temperate deciduous angiosperms.
