The dashed Cc-based curves do not quite follow Table I: fitting them gives ΔHa = 78.9 kJ mol⁻¹ for Kc (Table I: 80.99) and 24.7 kJ mol⁻¹ for Ko (23.72), with Ko at 25 °C 172 rather than 165.8 mmol mol⁻¹. The model is up to 4 % above the drawn Kc and 5 % below the drawn Ko at 40 °C. Since the same digitisation recovers the solid curves, the dashed curves appear to have been drawn from a slightly different fit than the one printed. Table I is kept: it is the printed parameter set, and the one adopted by Sharkey et al. (2007).

**Fig. 3 is not reproduced.** lgm (Eqn 13) is computed from the measured A/Ci curves of Bernacchi et al. (2001), refitted on a Cc basis at each temperature; neither those Cc-based Vc,max values nor the operating Ci are printed. With Bernacchi 2001's (Ci-based) Vc,max(T), gm = 0.0045 Vc,max at 25 °C and any single Ci between 150 and 300 µmol mol⁻¹, the model's lgm (Rd = 0) falls with temperature, or peaks at 15–20 °C, up to 35 °C, where Fig. 3 rises steadily from 0.09 to 0.17. A free ΔHa for Vc,max and a free Ci do not fit the figure either (residuals up to 0.04). The model still gives `A_inf` and `l_gm` by Eqn 13, but they are not validated. The text also disagrees with itself here: lgm at 10 °C is 0.08 in Results, 0.10 in the abstract and about 0.09 in Fig. 3.

**Eqn 13.** Read literally, "Acc and Aci ... using the actual gm and infinite gm, respectively" makes lgm negative. `l_gm` = (A_inf − A)/A_inf, with A_inf at Cc = Ci.

**Closed form.** Eqn 8 with Cc for Ci and Eqn 9 give a quadratic in A, as in Ethier & Livingston (2004): a = −1/gm, b = (Vc,max − Rd)/gm + Ci + Km, c = Rd (Ci + Km) − Vc,max (Ci − Γ*), Km = Kc (1 + O/Ko). The root (−b + √(b² − 4ac))/2a, the smaller one since a < 0, keeps Cc ≥ 0; it matches `brentq` on the implicit equation to 5 × 10⁻¹³ over 5–45 °C, Ci 20–1600 µmol mol⁻¹ and a range of gm, Vc,max and Rd. The other root has Cc below −Km. gm is per bar, so A/gm is in µbar, taken equal to µmol mol⁻¹ at 1 bar.

**Assumed parameters.** gm25 = 0.095 mol m⁻² s⁻¹ bar⁻¹ (variable-J method, Fig. 1 caption; the constant-J method gave 0.1075). Vc,max and Rd follow Bernacchi et al. (2001) Table 1. That Vc,max(T) is fitted on a Ci basis; the paper refits Vc,max on a Cc basis at each temperature but does not print it, so `A`, `A_inf` and `l_gm` combine a Ci-based Vc,max with Cc-based kinetics and are illustrative. Vc,max,25 = 21.1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ is gm25/0.0045, from the paper's gm = 0.0045 Vc,max; The ratio comes from carbon isotope data (Evans et al. 1986; von Caemmerer et al. 1994), not from these leaves. The 2001 functions give 0.995 (Vc,max) and 1.006 (Rd) at 25 °C, so Vc,max(25 °C) = 20.99. Rd25 is not given and defaults to 0. O = 210 mmol mol⁻¹. The fits cover 10–40 °C; the 5–45 °C slider range extrapolates (Fig. 2a is drawn over 5–45 °C).
