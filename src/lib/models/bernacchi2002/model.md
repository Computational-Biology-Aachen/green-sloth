The [Bernacchi 2002](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.008250) model adds the temperature response of the mesophyll conductance $g_m$ to the Rubisco-limited Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model. Measured in tobacco between 10 and 40 °C from gas exchange combined with chlorophyll fluorescence, $g_m$ rises with a $Q_{10}$ of about 2.2, peaks at 35–37.5 °C and falls above, which the paper fits with a peaked Arrhenius function (Eqn 6). With $g_m(T)$ known, the chloroplastic CO₂ $C_c = C_i - A/g_m$ (Eqn 9) replaces $C_i$, and the Michaelis constants $K_c$ and $K_o$ and the compensation point $\Gamma^*$ are recalculated on a $C_c$ basis (Table I). They come out 25–35 % ($K_c$) and 20–50 % ($K_o$) below the apparent, $C_i$-based values of [Bernacchi 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.2001.00668.x).

In GreenSloth the Rubisco-limited assimilation (Eqn 8, with $C_c$ for $C_i$) is solved together with Eqn 9 in closed form, the smaller root of a quadratic in $A$, at leaf temperature for antisense tobacco (_Nicotiana tabacum_) with low Rubisco. $V_{c,max}$ and $R_d$ follow Bernacchi 2001's temperature functions, as the paper does. The model also gives $A$ at infinite $g_m$ and the limitation by $g_m$, $l_{gm}$ (Eqn 13).
