The [Ethier & Livingston 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.2004.01140.x) model is the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model of C₃ photosynthesis with a finite CO₂ transfer (mesophyll) conductance $g_i$ between the intercellular airspaces and the chloroplasts. With $A = g_i(C_i - C_c)$ (Eqn 9), the Rubisco- and RuBP-limited rates at the chloroplast become quadratics in $C_i$ (Eqn 10): non-rectangular hyperbolae with the same asymptote $V_{c,max} - R_d$ and compensation point $\Gamma$ as the usual curves at $C_c$, but with less curvature the smaller $g_i$ is. The conventional rectangular hyperbolae at $C_i$ (Eqns 14 and 15) assume $g_i = \infty$, and the paper shows that fitting them to A–Ci curves of leaves with a low $g_i$ underestimates $V_{c,max}$, by up to 30 % or more.

GreenSloth evaluates both forms side by side, with $C_i$ and $g_i$ as the main inputs. The defaults are the "true" leaf the paper uses to generate its ideal A–Ci curves (Figs 4–6): $V_{c,max}$ = 56, $R_d$ = 0.6 and $J_{max}$ = 112 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at 1500 µmol quanta m⁻² s⁻¹, with the Rubisco kinetics of Jordan & Ogren (1984) at 25 °C and 210 mbar O₂. $\Gamma^*$ follows Rubisco's specificity factor through Eqn 5, so it changes with O₂, as in Figs 8 and 9. Eqn 16, the rectangular hyperbola with an apparent Michaelis–Menten constant $K_m(CO_2)_i$ that the paper proposes as a correction for $g_i$, is included with that constant's value for $g_i$ = 0.2.
