**Left out: the two-line fits.** Figs 3b,c, 4b,c and 6b–d plot the slopes $m_1$, $m_2$ and the intercepts $R_{Dark}$, '$R_{Light}$' of two straight lines fitted by least squares to each modelled A–I curve (0–100 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, transition $I_{trans}$ chosen by the minimum summed mean square of the two fits; Methods S1). The black circles marking $I_{trans}$ in Figs 2c, 3a, 4a, 5b and 6a come from the same fit. They are a regression over a scan, not model outputs, and the validations have no operation for it, so they are not in `validation.ts`. They were checked in a scratch script that applies the paper's fitting rule to the model's A on a 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ grid: Fig 3b at O = 400 mmol mol⁻¹ m₁ = 0.055, m₂ = 0.035; Fig 3c '$R_{Light}$'/$R_{Dark}$ has its minimum of 0.63 near 50 mmol mol⁻¹ and is 0.69 at 400; Fig 4b m₂ ≈ 0.003 at Ca = 50; Fig 4c 0.39 at Ca = 50, 0.66 at 400, 0.98 at 2000; Fig 6b $R_{Dark}$ = 2.74, '$R_{Light}$' = 2.17 at 40 °C; Fig 6c 0.25 at 10 °C and 0.79 at 40 °C. All lie on the paper's curves. Whether the boundary point belongs to both fits changes these by less than 0.005.

**Fig 2c's fitted lines** are drawn from the coefficients the text prints (phase 1: 0.0654x − 0.9688; phase 2: 0.0467x − 0.6383), not computed. An ordinary least-squares fit to the model's A at integer I below and above $I_c$ gives 0.0653x − 0.9687 and 0.0466x − 0.6384.

**Equations.** A–I curves use Eqn A9 (the smaller root of Eqns A6–A8, $g_t$ from Eqn 19), which is Eqn 14 with Eqn 15. The root agrees with brentq on the implicit Eqn A5 to 5e-13 over Ca = 0–2000 µmol mol⁻¹, O = 10–400 mmol mol⁻¹, T = 10–40 °C and I = 0.5–400; it is the root with Cc ≥ 0 (A between −Rd − I'/2 and min(I', gt·Ca)). The model works in mole fractions (C/P in µmol mol⁻¹), so the paper's AP/g is A/g with A in µmol and g in mol m⁻² s⁻¹. For the Laisk approach (Fig 7) the paper replaces Ca by Ci and $r_b + r_s + r_m$ by $r_m$ in Eqns 15 and A7–A9: `A_i` is Eqn A9 with Ci and gm. The paper doesn't say how gm behaves there; it is taken as gm = 2.5 gt(I) (Fig 2a's gm), so it still rises with irradiance. With that, Fig 7 reproduces, including its intercept.

**Conductances (Fig 2a).** gm = 1.5 gs and gsw = 1.6 gs with the boundary layer ignored, so 1/gt = 1/gs + 1/gm: gs = (5/3) gt, gm = 2.5 gt, gsw = (8/3) gt. At I = 100 this gives the text's gt = 0.020, gsw = 0.053 and gm = 0.050 mol m⁻² s⁻¹.

**Temperature (Fig 6).** Rd follows Heskel et al. (2016), ln Rd = a + 0.1012 T − 0.0005 T². The paper doesn't print a; it is set by Rd = 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at 25 °C (Table 1), which gives Rd = 0.285 at 10 °C and 2.80 at 40 °C, and fitted intercepts $R_{Dark}$ of 0.26 and 2.74 as in Fig 6b. Γ* = exp(c − ΔHa/(R Tk)) in Pa with c = 20.07 and ΔHa = 46.3 kJ mol⁻¹ (Walker et al. 2013). R = 8.314 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and Tk = T + 273.15 are assumed (not printed). This gives Γ*/P = 40.22 µmol mol⁻¹ at 25 °C and P = 100 kPa, Table 1's 40.2. The paper doesn't say how Γ* changes with O for Figs 3 and 5; it is taken as proportional to O (Eqn 5), scaled from O/P = 210 mmol mol⁻¹, the condition of Table 1. Table 1 prints gt = 0.001 + (1 − f)I/(3680 × 10⁻⁶), which is dimensionally garbled; the model follows Eqn 19, which gives the text's gt = 0.020 at I = 100.

**Text values (simplest version).** With the model's Γ* = 40.22: Ic = 15.26 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (text 15.2, Eqn 16); at I₀.₅ = 2Ic, A = 0.52 and m₂ = 0.034 (text 0.52 and 0.034); Eqn 17 gives 47.8, 35.9, 31.9, 29.9 and 23.9 % for n = 2, 3, 4, 5 and ∞ (text 48, 36, 32, 30 … 24 %), and 28.2 % for n = 6.6 (text 28.1 %, from 6.6/5.6 · 23.8 %). The Discussion's 30 % inhibition of Rd below Ic gives a compensation point of 10.7 by Eqn 16 (the text prints 10.5), consistent with its own phase-1 slope 0.0938 ≈ 1/10.66; the 10.5 is presumably rounded differently. That scenario has no figure and is not built in.
