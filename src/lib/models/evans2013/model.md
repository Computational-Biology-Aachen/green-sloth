The [Evans & von Caemmerer 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.2012.02591.x) model explains the temperature response of the mesophyll conductance $g_m$, the conductance that sets the chloroplastic CO₂ $C_c = C_i - A/g_m$ in finite-$g_m$ versions of the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model. Measured in tobacco by carbon isotope discrimination with the ternary corrections of Farquhar & Cernusak (2012), $g_m$ rises linearly with leaf temperature between 15 and 40 °C, by about 5 % of its 25 °C value per °C, in both 2 and 21 % O₂ (Fig. 5). The paper then splits $g_m$ into two conductances in series (Eqn 13): diffusion through the liquid phase of cell wall, cytosol and stroma, $g_{liq} = \rho H D S_c / l$ (Eqns 14–16), which falls slightly with temperature because CO₂ solubility drops faster than its diffusivity rises, and diffusion across membranes, $g_{mem}$, given an assumed activation energy of 50 kJ mol⁻¹ (Eqn 17). Their combination rises with temperature much like the measurements.

In GreenSloth the model gives $g_{liq}$, $g_{mem}$ and $g_m$ at leaf temperature for tobacco (_Nicotiana tabacum_), with the path length, chloroplast surface area, membrane conductance and its activation energy as sliders, and, for comparison, the measured linear response of Fig. 5. It does not compute assimilation; the isotope equations the paper uses to estimate $g_m$ from gas exchange (Eqns 2–11, A1–A20) are not included.
