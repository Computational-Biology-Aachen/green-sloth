The [Bernacchi 2001](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.2001.00668.x) model provides in vivo temperature response functions for the parameters of Rubisco-limited photosynthesis in the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model. Using transgenic tobacco with only about 10 % of the normal Rubisco content, so that photosynthesis stays Rubisco-limited over a wide range of CO₂, Bernacchi et al. measured gas exchange between 10 and 40 °C at different CO₂ and O₂ concentrations and derived the temperature responses of the CO₂ compensation point $\Gamma^*$, the Michaelis constants $K_c$ and $K_o$, the maximum carboxylation and oxygenation rates $V_{c,max}$ and $V_{o,max}$, and day respiration $R_d$. Each follows $\exp(c - \Delta H_a/RT_k)$, with the scaling constants and activation energies of the paper's Table 1; $V_{c,max}$, $V_{o,max}$ and $R_d$ are relative to their values at 25 °C.

These functions replaced earlier in vitro based ones and became the standard temperature parameterisation of C₃ leaf models, including [Bernacchi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.12118). In GreenSloth the Rubisco-limited net assimilation $A = (1 - \Gamma^*/C_i)\,v_c - R_d$ is computed at leaf temperature from these functions, together with the carboxylation and oxygenation velocities. The defaults are the conditions of the paper's lemon test (Fig. 3).
