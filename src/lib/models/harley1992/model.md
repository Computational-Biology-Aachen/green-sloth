The [Harley 1992](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-3040.1992.tb00974.x) model parameterises the Farquhar, von Caemmerer & Berry (1980) model of C₃ photosynthesis for cotton grown at ambient (35 Pa) and elevated (65 Pa) CO₂. To fit the gas exchange data it adds a limitation by triose phosphate utilisation (TPU) to the Rubisco- and RuBP-regeneration-limited rates, so that net assimilation is $A = (1 - 0.5\,O/\tau C_i)\min(W_c, W_j, W_p) - R_d$. The Rubisco kinetic constants $K_c$, $K_o$ and the specificity factor $\tau$ follow Arrhenius functions, while $V_{c,max}$, $J_{max}$ and TPU follow a function with high-temperature deactivation (Eqn 9), each fitted separately for the two growth treatments. Electron transport follows a non-rectangular light response with a fixed quantum efficiency.

The biochemical model is coupled to the Ball, Woodrow & Berry (1987) model of stomatal conductance, which together determine the intercellular CO₂ at a given ambient CO₂. Growth at elevated CO₂ changed the photosynthetic capacity of cotton only slightly, so that assimilation at the growth conditions was about 25 % higher despite a lower stomatal conductance. In GreenSloth $C_i$ drives the model, and the ambient CO₂ and stomatal conductance that give it are computed from Eqns 10 and 11; the defaults are the growth conditions of the paper's Fig. 8 with the parameters for plants grown at 35 Pa CO₂.
