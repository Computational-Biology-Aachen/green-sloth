Figs 9.2, 9.4, 9.5, 9.6 and 9.7 are reproduced. Fig 9.1 is a stoichiometric schematic without values, and Figs 9.3 and 9.8 show measurements only (Fig 9.8's Ja/4 is calculated from measured A). Measured points are not drawn, as they are not tabulated.

**Equations.** Eqns 9.28 and 9.29 are printed with `+ Rd` in the coefficient of the linear term. Substituting Eqn 9.27 into Eqns 9.9 and 9.14 gives `− Rd`, the form whose smaller root tends to Eqns 9.9 and 9.14 as gi → ∞ (and Ethier & Livingston 2004's Eqn 10); the model uses `− Rd`. The smaller root agrees with a numerical root-find of A = Ac(Ci − A/gi) to 1e-8 over Ci = 20–1600 µbar and gi = 0.05–3; the printed `+ Rd` form is off by up to 1.4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. Eqn 9.24 is printed `Topt = H[S + R ln(H/E − 1)]`; it is `H / [S + R ln(H/E − 1)]`, the maximum of Eqn 9.23 (34.1 and 31.2 °C for the two parameter sets of Fig 9.4, where the curves peak). Eqns 9.22 and 9.23 are used as printed, with R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, 298 K and 273 + t. Eqn 9.21 (min of the A's) is used, as the chapter gives it for C > Γ*. Below Γ* (about 37 µbar) it differs from Eqn 9.20, whose negative factor (1 − Γ*/C) selects the largest rate instead; the finite-gi quadratics have no W form to take the minimum of. The figures only clip curves there, but the model's A and A_inf below Γ* follow Eqn 9.21, not Eqn 9.20. The Rubisco activation function of Sellers et al. (Eqn 9.26) and the temperature function of June et al. (Eqn 9.25) are cited alternatives that the chapter does not build into its model or figures; they are left out.

**Γ\*.** Figs 9.5 and 9.7 are drawn with Γ* = 36.9 µbar, Table 9.1's bracketed value from Bernacchi et al. (2002), not its own 38.6 µbar (γ* = 0.0001935): with 38.6 the plateaus of Fig 9.5b sit 0.2 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ low (Ac at c = 100 and 250 µbar: 6.54 and 20.12 against the drawn 6.75 and 20.3) and the Aj curve at c = 100 0.6 low. With 36.9 µbar every curve of Fig 9.5 and the demand function of Fig 9.7 lie on the figure (read at 400 dpi, to about 0.05 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹). The model's default γ* is therefore 0.0001845 (36.9 µbar at 200 mbar O₂); the other kinetic constants are Table 9.1's unbracketed values (Kc = 260 µbar, Ko = 179 mbar); the bracketed Kc and Ko do not match the figure. Γ*'s activation energy is the bracketed 24.6 kJ mol⁻¹ that goes with the bracketed Γ*; the other activation energies are Table 9.1's unbracketed ones. No figure is away from 25 °C except Fig 9.4, which uses only Jmax, so this choice affects no figure.

**Fig 9.5** uses Table 9.1 (Vcmax = 80, Jmax = 160, Rd = 1 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), 25 °C, 200 mbar O₂, θ = 0.7, abs = 0.85 and f = 0.15 (Eqns 9.15, 9.16). Its x-axis is the chloroplastic pCO₂, so the curves are the gi = ∞ forms (Eqns 9.9, 9.14, 9.21) with C in the place of Ci. The chapter draws Ac and Aj only where they are extensions of A; here they are drawn over the whole range, under the solid A.

**Fig 9.4.** Eqn 9.23 with the legend's E, H and S, and with Table 9.1's E, H and S (Farquhar et al. 1980), both at 50 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ at 25 °C. The fit is drawn solid over the measured range (7.5–37.5 °C, the first and last data points) and dotted beyond it, as in the chapter.

**Fig 9.2.** The lines are adapted from von Caemmerer et al. (1994) and their parameters are not printed. With Table 9.1's kinetics they were fitted to the lines read off the figure (Eqn 9.9 or 9.14 at Ci, 1000 µmol quanta m⁻² s⁻¹): wild type Ac with Vcmax = 90.9 and Rd = 3.71, wild type Aj with Jmax = 182.2 and Rd = 1.51 (J = 147 at this irradiance), anti-Rubisco Ac with Vcmax = 37.4 and Rd = 2.27 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. The lines stay within 0.85 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ of the figure. A single Rd for both wild-type lines fits worse (up to 1.4), so the drawn lines were presumably not computed from one consistent parameter set.

**Fig 9.6.** The L335 line with the legend's values matches the figure to 0.04 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. The _R. rubrum_ line with the legend's values lies 0.15–0.4 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ below the drawn one, increasingly so at high Ci; no rounding of the printed values explains it, and the difference is under 1 % of the axis. The legend gives Γ* in µbar at 200 mbar O₂; it is entered through γ* = Γ*/O. The wild type's parameters are not printed: with Table 9.1's kinetics, Vcmax = 83.5 and Rd = 5.45 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ were fitted to its line (within 0.13). That Rd is implausibly high for tobacco, so the line was probably drawn with other kinetics; with Rd = 1 the line fits equally well with Kc ≈ 420 µbar (Vcmax = 97.7), but no such Kc appears in the chapter.

**Fig 9.7.** The demand function is Fig 9.5a's curve at 1000 µmol quanta m⁻² s⁻¹. The supply function's g and Ca are not printed and were fitted to the dashed line read off the figure: g = 0.2 mol m⁻² s⁻¹ bar⁻¹ and Ca = 383 µbar (fit: 0.1995 and 382.8). They meet the demand function at Ci = 273.2 µbar, A = 21.95, where the figure draws the drop line (272.5 µbar, 22.1).
