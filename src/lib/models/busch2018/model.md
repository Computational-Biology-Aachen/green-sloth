[Busch, Sage & Farquhar (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41477-017-0065-x) extend the FvCB model with de novo nitrogen assimilation through the photorespiratory pathway. A fraction $\alpha_G$ of the photorespiratory carbon leaves the cycle as glycine and a fraction $\alpha_S$ as serine, instead of returning to the chloroplast. Glycine export lowers the CO₂ released per oxygenation, so $\Gamma^*_{\alpha_G} = 0.5(1-\alpha_G)O/S_{c/o}$ (Eqn 12). The electrons needed to reduce NO₃⁻ raise the cost of carboxylation in $W_j$ (Eqn 13). Under a TPU limitation, the exported carbon adds a second carbon sink, so $W_p = 3T_p/(1 - 0.5(1 + 3\alpha_G + 4\alpha_S)\Phi)$ (Eqn 14) and A rises with photorespiration. $\alpha_G$ and $\alpha_S$ aren't constants. Each is the smaller of an upper bound ($\alpha_G^{max}$, $\alpha_S^{max}$) and the value at which the N exported reaches the maximum supply $N^{max}$, with a closed form for each limitation (Eqns 15–21). This replaces the single $\alpha$ of Harley & Sharkey (1991).

The model is fitted to A–Cᵢ curves of KNO₃-fertilised sunflower (_Helianthus annuus_) at 25 °C, with mesophyll conductance taken as infinite (C = Cᵢ). The defaults are the fit of Fig 3a (Vcmax = 103.9, J = 185.2, Tp = 11.5, Rd = 1.29 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, $\alpha_G^{max}$ = 0.09, $\alpha_S^{max}$ = 0.38, $N^{max}$ = 1.21 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, O = 19.7 kPa, i.e. 21 % at the site's ~94 kPa). CO₂ is in Pa and O₂ in kPa. The paper doesn't print the Rubisco kinetics. GreenSloth uses the 25 °C values of the fitting tool the paper used (Sharkey et al. 2007).
