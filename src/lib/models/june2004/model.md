The [June 2004](https://doi.org/10.1071/FP03250) model is a simple equation for the reversible temperature dependence of photosynthetic electron transport. Measuring the electron transport rate $J$ of intact soybean leaves by chlorophyll fluorescence between 15 and 45 °C, June, Evans & Farquhar found that the decline above the optimum is fully reversible and symmetric, and replaced the modified Arrhenius function with activation, deactivation and entropy terms (Farquhar et al. 1980; Harley et al. 1992) by a Gaussian: $J(T_L) = J(T_o)\,e^{-((T_L - T_o)/\Omega)^2}$. Its three parameters are the rate at the optimum, $J(T_o)$, the optimum temperature, $T_o$, and the width $\Omega$, the distance from $T_o$ at which $J$ falls to $e^{-1}$ of its optimum.

The paper fits the equation to soybean grown at different temperatures, CO₂ concentrations and nitrate supplies, and to published responses of many herbaceous and tree species. Nitrogen supply changed $J(T_o)$, growth temperature shifted $T_o$, and $\Omega$ stayed near 18 °C, increasing slightly with $T_o$ across species. In GreenSloth the equation is implemented with the leaf of Fig. 1 as the default, together with the photochemical efficiency of PSII it was derived from, $J = \phi_{PSII}\,I\,a(1-f)/2$, and the response normalised to 1 at 25 °C, the form in which it enters leaf photosynthesis models such as [Bernacchi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.12118).
