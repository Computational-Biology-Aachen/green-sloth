[Harley & Sharkey (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00035838) extend the triose phosphate utilisation (TPU) limitation of the FvCB model (Sharkey 1985) to explain reversed sensitivity to O₂ and CO₂: when phosphate limits, net assimilation often rises with O₂ and falls with CO₂, while the classic $W_p = 3T + 0.5\,v_o$ makes it insensitive to both. If less than the maximal three-quarters of the carbon lost as glycolate returns to the chloroplast as glycerate (some remains in the cytosol, e.g. as amino acids), the glycerate kinase reaction sequesters less phosphate, and photorespiration releases phosphate in the stroma. The TPU-limited carboxylation rate then becomes $W_p = 3T + 2(1 - f_{gly})\,v_o$ (Eqns 5, 6, 6b), with $f_{gly}$ the returning fraction; with $v_o = \Phi\,v_c$ it has the closed form $W_p = 3T/(1 - 2(1 - f_{gly})\Phi)$, so the paper's iteration is not needed.

The model is fitted to A–Ci curves of cotton (_Gossypium hirsutum_, 29 °C) and soybean (_Glycine max_, 25 °C) at 20 and ambient mbar O₂, with three returning fractions (3/4, 0, 1/2); half returning fits soybean best. The defaults are the soybean fit of Fig 2C (Vcmax = 120.9, R = 49.6, T = 9.62 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, PFD 1500, 180 mbar O₂). CO₂ is in µbar and O₂ in mbar. The paper doesn't print the Rubisco kinetics or the light response; GreenSloth takes them from Harley et al. (1992), on the same cotton, which reproduces every figure.
